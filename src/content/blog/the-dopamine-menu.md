There's a moment almost every person in early recovery knows by heart. The restlessness creeps in. The house feels too quiet, or too loud. Something in the body says *I need to feel different right now* — and for years, alcohol was the fastest way to answer that call.

Cravings aren't a character flaw. They're biology. Alcohol hijacks the brain's dopamine system, and when it's gone, the brain still goes looking for that quick hit of relief. The problem was never that you wanted to feel good. The problem was that the menu was too short.

So at Beyond BARS Coaching, we built a longer one.

## What Is the Dopamine Menu?

The Dopamine Menu is exactly what it sounds like: a menu of ways to feel good that won't cost you your recovery. Instead of one option (drink), you get an entire menu of real, accessible ways to shift your state — organized the way a restaurant menu is, so that in a hard moment, you're not standing in your kitchen trying to invent a coping skill from scratch. You're just choosing from the menu in front of you.

**Appetizers** — quick resets, under a few minutes: a 5-minute gratitude journal, ice water with lemon, three slow stretches, a text to your sponsor or support person, a brain dump on paper, stepping outside for fresh air.

**Entrees** — the more nourishing, time-in options: a yoga or movement class, a call to someone who matters to you, time in the garden, a hike, cooking something new, reading a chapter, showing up to a meeting or coaching session, making something with your hands.

**Sides** — the pairings, for when you're avoiding a task: music while you clean, a podcast during errands, journaling alongside a skincare ritual, a show while you fold laundry.

**Desserts** — enjoy sparingly: the reality TV marathon, the scrolling, the online shopping. Not off-limits. Just named honestly, so you can choose them on purpose instead of falling into them by default.

**Specials** — occasional, and worth planning for: a day trip, a date night, a game night, live music, a massage, a guilt-free half day off.

The point isn't to white-knuckle your way past a craving. It's to have somewhere real to go instead.

## Why This Matters for an Alcohol-Free Life

Early recovery asks a lot of the nervous system. You're not just removing a substance — you're removing the thing that used to regulate mood, mark celebrations, soften hard days, and fill unstructured time, often all at once. If nothing replaces that function, the craving doesn't quiet down. It gets louder.

This is where the Dopamine Menu earns its place inside our Three Pillars framework — Nourishment, Self-Care, and Personal Development. Every item on the menu maps back to one of these pillars, which means reaching for the menu isn't a detour from your recovery plan. It *is* your recovery plan, in action, in real time.

It also connects directly to the FAST Method we use with clients — Foundation, Accountability, Support, Tools. The Dopamine Menu is a Tool in the truest sense: something concrete you can hold onto the moment a craving shows up, before you have to think too hard about what to do next. That's the whole design. A craving is not the time for a brainstorm. It's the time for a menu you already know by heart.

## How We Use It in Beyond BARS Coaching

Inside our coaching and community work, the Dopamine Menu shows up in a few practical ways:

- In **private coaching sessions**, we help you build a personalized version — one that reflects what actually lights you up, not a generic list. What's on your Entrees? What's genuinely restorative for you, versus what you think should be?
- In **group meetings and yoga sessions**, we practice pulling from the menu together, so it becomes muscle memory rather than a piece of paper in a drawer somewhere.
- In **journal therapy work**, we use prompts to explore why certain cravings show up when they do — what need is actually underneath them — so the menu item you choose is treating the real hunger, not just distracting from it.
- In the **Sober Rebel Club community**, members share what's working on their own menus, which means you're never building this alone. Someone else's Appetizer might become your new favorite.
