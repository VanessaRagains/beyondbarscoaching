I recently completed my RYT 300 through Ignite #Yoga and was taught by BIPOC (Black Indigenous People of Color) Instructors. We explored the true history of #YogaCulture and how the West has stripped this sacred practice of its true purpose.

My Instructor, Susanna Barataki (she/her) shares, "At its root yoga is a structured, scientific framework and embodiment practice that aims at curing our personal and social ills." I have this definition underlined in my copy of her book, "Embrace Yoga's Roots" and that is not the only area I highlighted, underlined, circled, or added notes in the margins. This book changed the way I view my role as a #Yoga Instructor and the way that I look at my personal #Yoga practice.

During the course, we explored the #Yamas and #Niyamas of #Yoga. We learned how to live a Yoga lifestyle beyond Asana (physical postures) and beyond our mats. These were the missing pieces to my practice.

I want to explore one of the #Yamas with you called, #Ahimsa. #Ahimsa is practicing non-violence and non-harm to yourself, people, and the environment. AKA: #SelfLove! Let's walk through how this could look. I'll use my experience as our example.

Now, take a moment to focus on how you may have caused yourself harm? For me, I think of my negative self-talk as causing harm to my mindset, confidence, and vitality.

Practicing #Ahimsa can look like, catching the negative self-talk and replacing it with something kind about the situation you're thinking negatively about.

For example, if I forget something at the store, or forget to take the dogs food out to thaw in time for her next meal, I get impatient with myself. I feel like I should have used my time more wisely or maybe I had too many thoughts in the moment and simply forgot.

It comes down to my relationship with time and perfection. #Yoga encourages a healthy relationship with time and zero emphasis on perfection — that's only how we ignorantly define Yoga in the West. But I'll dive into that topic in another post.

Here are ways I am learning how to change my relationship with time and perfection by practicing Ahimsa. WARNING: Tough-Coaching-Love ahead!

**1. Daily Check-ins** — I talk to myself on a regular basis. I'll say, "Hey Vanessa, How are you feeling in your body today?" and pay attention to any sensations that I feel.

Where do you feel that sensation? I feel emotions in my gut, heart, and throat. Learning where you feel and store your emotions will give you insights on how to work through them.

Go ahead and try it!

**2. Nourishment** — Is what you are fueling your body with causing any harm? Be honest with yourself because I will share my honest opinion, it's my blog, of course!

Fast food is NOT nourishment.

There is not a *healthy* type of soda.

Nourishment is an area that humans cause continual harm to their own body. Most common health concerns are related to what you fuel your body with.

**3. Self-Care** — I define Self-Care as rituals, maintaining a spiritual practice, in my case, its #Yoga, and movement that gets your heart rate up (depending on your state of physical health).

Adding self-care into your life can seem overwhelming, especially depending on your responsibilities. So, we can start small, even tiny if that feels more comfortable.

Something tiny could be reflecting on 2–5 things that you are grateful for in that moment.

A small way to bring self-care into your day could be repeating a positive phrase, like a mantra while placing your hands on your heart. The mantra could be, "I am capable of accomplishing anything I put my mind to."

Medium self-care could look like creating a daily ritual to start your day. I have a short video that shows you the [3 simple steps to create your own ritual](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Sz6P5NRnkQw).

Your self-care routine can grow into a full day of self-care to experiencing a 5-day retreat in Puerta Vallarta!

Choose the level of self-care you will start with and build from there.

**4. Personal Development** — As we grow wiser, our experiences shape how we think and evolve.

Something that I have been working on is creating healthy boundaries with myself. You may be thinking, "I thought boundaries are made to support our interactions with other people?" That is true, however, I believe that we have to start creating boundaries with ourselves, first.

This could look like setting a goal to develop a habit of meditation.

Side note, I recently read and studied "You Are A Badass" by Jen Sincero for the 7th time and am finally implementing meditation into my day. My #Meditation practice consists of me setting my timer for 15-min, lighting two candles on my alter, striking my bell, focusing on 10 things I am grateful for, taking a breath, then repeating the 4 major upgrades I am actively working towards right now. I pause between to state that "I am so in love with this life, I am so in love with this life. I am so grateful for this life, I am so grateful for this life" before continuing to repeat what I am bringing into my life.

My biggest AhHa! from my new practice is how I set the tone for my day. I start out with gratitude which means that I approach each part of my day with gratitude. Before I know it, I am hearing the gentle chime to tell me that 15-minutes has passed by and it's time to start the day. It always goes by faster than I realize.

You can get creative by switching to repeating a mantra, or choose to practice sitting in silence while you observe your thoughts. I like to start with the structure and then play with switching it up. Building this habit and holding myself accountable to my meditation practice builds trust with myself. Developing a trusting relationship with myself has been a game-changer. Through this practice, the trust I have built has evolved into getting comfortable saying no when I do not want to participate in an outing or deciding who I spend my time engaging with.

Trust me, it's worth it!

I hope this discussion provides accessible ways for you to begin practicing Ahimsa (non-harm) with yourself starting today. Be sure to save this post for referencing later on.

Below is a #Journaling exercise for you to work through.

#### REBEL ACTION STEPS:

- **Set an alarm on your phone for your daily check-in with yourself.** Ask, "Hey , how are you feeling in your body today?" Be honest. You do not have to speak out loud. You can do this exercise in your head.
  - Where do I feel these emotions in my body?
  - Actively breathe into those areas. Inhale to the count of 3, exhale to the count of 6. This is called the Ratio breath.
- **Are the foods you are fueling your body with creating harm?** Be honest with yourself. Write them down. Eliminate them from your diet.
- **How do you want to incorporate Self-Care into your life?** As a ritual? Starting a spiritual practice? Choosing to find healthy movement that gets your heart rate up? Ultimately, exploring what this will look like for you.
- **What is 1 boundary you want to create in your life?** How will sticking to this boundary help you develop a trusting relationship with yourself?

This only scratches the surface of practicing #Ahimsa. These are some helpful ideas to start practicing #Ahimsa today.
