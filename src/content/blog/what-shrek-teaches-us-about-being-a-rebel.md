This Friday, 5/15, Shrek celebrates its 25th Anniversary in theaters, and I'm more excited than I should probably admit.

But the older I get, the more I realize Shrek wasn't just a funny movie with talking donkeys and swamp jokes. It's actually a story about identity, authenticity, boundaries, healing, friendship, and learning to stop shrinking yourself to fit into someone else's version of "acceptable."

Which sounds… very Rebel to me.

**1. Shrek Stops Performing for Approval**

At the beginning of the movie, Shrek comes across as tough, grumpy, and emotionally unavailable because the world has already decided who he is.

People fear him before they know him.

Judge him before speaking to him.

Assume the worst before seeing his heart.

So he leans into the role.

But underneath all that swamp-protecting sarcasm is someone who actually wants peace, connection, and acceptance.

A lot of Rebels know this feeling:

Building armor.

Using humor.

Pretending not to care.

Acting "fine."

Keeping people at a distance because it feels safer.

Shrek teaches us that healing often begins when we stop performing and allow ourselves to be fully seen.

Messy. Complicated. Human. Real.

**2. Your Swamp Is Allowed to Have Boundaries**

One of the funniest parts of *Shrek* is how serious he is about his swamp.

"Get outta my swamp!" Iconic, but there's wisdom there.

Shrek understands something many people struggle with:

Not everyone gets unlimited access to your space, energy, peace, or nervous system.

Rebels learn this, too.

Protecting your peace isn't selfish.

Needing solitude isn't failure.

Creating boundaries isn't being mean.

Sometimes growth looks less like becoming "nicer" and more like becoming honest about what drains you and what restores you.

**3. Donkey Represents the Power of Connection**

Donkey never gives up on Shrek.

He annoys him.

Challenges him.

Makes him laugh.

Keeps showing up.

And eventually, Shrek softens.

That's the thing about healing:

Isolation feels safer.

Connection helps us grow anyway.

Every Rebel needs people who can sit beside them in the swamp without trying to "fix" them.

People who can hold space for both the chaos and the humor.

People who remind us that life can still be playful.

People who help us laugh when things feel heavy.

Community matters.

**4. Fiona Refuses to Become Smaller to Be Loved**

Princess Fiona spent years believing she had to transform into something more "acceptable" to deserve love.

But the ending flips the entire fairytale narrative:

Her true form was never the problem.

That message hits differently as an adult.

So many people spend years trying to become more digestible:

Less emotional.

Less intense.

Less outspoken.

Less weird.

Less ambitious.

Less sensitive.

Rebels eventually realize:

The goal isn't to make it easier for the world to consume. The goal is to become more fully yourself.

**5. The Villain Is Obsessed With Perfection**

Lord Farquaad wants a perfect kingdom.

Perfect appearances.

Perfect image.

Perfect control.

But everything about him is disconnected from authenticity.

Meanwhile, the swamp is messy, imperfect, emotional, unpredictable, and alive.

That's real life.

Rebel growth isn't about becoming flawless.

It's about becoming honest.

Grounded.

Self-aware.

Authentic.

A little chaotic sometimes?

Sure.

But real.

**Final Thought: Rebels Belong in the Swamp Too**

Maybe that's why Shrek still resonates 25 years later.

It reminds us that people who feel different, misunderstood, emotional, unconventional, or out of place still deserve love, belonging, friendship, adventure, and joy.

You don't have to become polished to be worthy.

You don't have to fit the kingdom's standards.

You don't have to pretend to be Prince Charming.

Sometimes the most meaningful life gets built in the swamp —

with healthy boundaries, loyal friends, healing laughter, and the courage to finally be yourself.

And that feels pretty Rebel to me.
