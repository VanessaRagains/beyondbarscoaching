### Science Made Simple

So, what exactly is cortisol?

Cortisol is your body's built-in alarm system. It's released when your brain thinks you're in danger, giving you energy and focus to respond. In short bursts, cortisol is helpful. It's what gets you moving in the morning or helps you react quickly in an emergency.

But here's the problem: chronic stress or repeated drinking keeps cortisol levels high. High cortisol levels over time can leave you anxious, exhausted, and feeling foggy.

Now, here's where alcohol comes in. Many people drink because they feel stressed or anxious, thinking alcohol will "take the edge off." And in the short term, it does dull the feeling — but behind the scenes, alcohol actually tells your body to release **more cortisol.**

That means the next day — or even just a few hours later — your stress response is cranked up, leaving you more anxious, more restless, and craving another drink. It's a vicious cycle.

Sobriety, on the other hand, helps break this loop. Within days to weeks of not drinking, cortisol levels begin to stabilize. Your nervous system relearns how to **turn off the alarm** naturally — without alcohol pulling the strings.

### Coaching Connection

So, how do we connect this science to your recovery journey?

This week in the FAST Method, we focus on **Accountability.**

Why accountability? Because noticing your stress triggers — and how you respond — is the first step in breaking the cortisol cycle.

I'll share an example. I used to think pouring a drink was my "stress relief." After a long day, it felt like the only way to shut my brain off. But in reality, alcohol was fueling the very stress I was trying to escape.

When I had accountability — whether that was journaling, a check-in with a coach, or just tracking my stress patterns — I could see the truth. And once I saw it, I could choose healthier ways to calm my system.

Accountability pulls back the curtain. It helps you see that the chaos isn't who you are — it's just your brain and body running a stressed-out pattern. And patterns can be changed.

### Practical Tool

This week's tool is the **Craving Pause Ritual.**

Next time you feel stress rising or a craving coming on, try this:

1. **Stop + Breathe.** Take three slow, deep breaths.
2. **Move.** Walk, stretch, or shake out your body for 60 seconds.
3. **Reflect.** Write down or say out loud: *"I'm feeling stressed. Alcohol will make this worse. Instead, I choose ________."*

This ritual interrupts the cortisol-craving loop. Every time you pause instead of pouring, you teach your brain a new pathway — one that leads to calm, not chaos.
