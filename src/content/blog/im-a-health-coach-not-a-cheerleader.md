I became a certified Health & Life Coach based on my own experience with working with one. I paid for 1 Coaching call per month which included sharing what I was working on, any obstacles that got in my way, even exploring why I wanted to become a Health Coach.

I've had my share of therapy. From the age of 6 until age 19, I worked with several Therapists to work through the traumas that I had experienced in my life. The work is different. You have to dissect your past until you are raw with emotion. I had therapy sessions where I just sat and expressed my emotions, I cried, screamed, and yelled through them. I even had an incredible therapist who bought a plastic bat, took me to a room with just a punching bag in it and I was given permission to beat the shit out of the punching bag. I took full advantage of that opportunity. Let's just say that plastic yellow bat was destroyed and I felt loads better.

I'll say it again. The work you do in therapy is different than the work you do with a Health and Life Coach.

Here are some ways that they differ:

**Therapy** focuses on healing past wounds, processing trauma, diagnosing mental health conditions, and treating psychological disorders. You go deep into your history to understand how it shaped you.

**Health and Life Coaching** focuses on the present and future. We start from where you are *right now* and build a roadmap to where you want to go. Coaching is action-oriented, goal-focused, and accountability-driven.

A Health Coach is not a therapist. I do not diagnose, treat, or counsel. What I do is:

- Help you identify what you truly want for your health and life.
- Create a step-by-step plan to get there.
- Hold you accountable to showing up for yourself.
- Celebrate your wins — even the small ones.
- Challenge you when you are playing small.

That last point is where the "not a cheerleader" part comes in.

A cheerleader cheers no matter what. A coach holds you to a higher standard — with compassion, not criticism.

I will call you in when you go off track. I will ask the hard questions. I will push you to be honest with yourself when you want to take the easy way out.

Because that's what real growth requires.

If you are wondering whether therapy or coaching is right for you — or whether you need both — I encourage you to explore both options. Many of my clients have a therapist *and* a coach, and they find that the two practices complement each other beautifully.
