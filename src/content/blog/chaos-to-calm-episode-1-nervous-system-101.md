## Science Made Simple

So, let's dive into today's topic: **Nervous System 101.**

Your nervous system is like the command center of your body. It controls how you react to stress, danger, or even just everyday challenges. And when it senses a threat, it has four common responses:

- **Fight** — snapping in anger, feeling irritable, or needing to control everything.
- **Flight** — anxiety, restlessness, or wanting to escape situations.
- **Freeze** — shutting down, feeling numb, zoning out.
- **Fawn** — people-pleasing, saying "yes" when you want to say "no," or over-accommodating to keep the peace.

Now, here's where alcohol comes in: when you drink, it doesn't actually fix your stress response. It numbs it.

Imagine your nervous system is like a fire alarm. When stress or anxiety hits, the alarm goes off. Alcohol doesn't put out the fire — it just cuts the wires to the alarm for a few hours. The problem is, when the alcohol wears off, the fire alarm goes off even louder. That's why stress, irritability, and anxiety often feel worse after drinking.

Over time, alcohol trains your body to stay stuck in chaos — fight, flight, freeze, or fawn. Sobriety, on the other hand, gives your nervous system the chance to **repair those alarm wires** and learn how to regulate naturally again.

## Coaching Connection

So how do we start this process of regulation?

That foundation starts with awareness. You can't change what you don't notice.

But here's the thing: once I could name it — *"I'm in flight right now"* — I had a choice. I could pause, breathe, and respond differently. And every time I chose calm over chaos, my nervous system got just a little stronger.

## Practical Tool

So here's your tool for this week: **The Daily Nervous System Check-In.**

At least once a day — maybe in the morning or before bed — ask yourself:

- Am I in **fight, flight, freeze, or fawn** right now?
- Take 3 slow, deep breaths. Inhale for 4, hold for 4, exhale for 6.
- Then write down just **one word** that describes how you're feeling.

That's it. Simple, but powerful.

This exercise builds awareness, and awareness is the foundation for change. Over time, your brain starts rewiring new pathways that connect stress to calm, instead of stress to drinking.
