Tomorrow, many of us will be gathering with our families to enjoy an incredible feast during the Thanksgiving holiday — a celebration built on a misleading narrative. This reality bothers me.

Even though my partner and I will be preparing four main dishes, including the turkey, and our guests will arrive at 3 PM, I feel compelled to emphasize the importance of supporting our Indigenous community to help restore our connection to Mother Earth.

Initially, I planned to create a "Land Acknowledgment" video post for social media. However, I realized it was essential to do some research first, and I'm glad I did! In the [**"Beyond Land Acknowledgment Explainer Video"**](https://nativegov.org/resources/beyond-land-acknowledgment-explainer-video/) that I found, non-Indigenous people are encouraged to create an action plan instead of simply posting a Land Acknowledgment. This prompted me to delete my original script and reflect on how I can take **meaningful action**. What does that look like for me? If you're curious about what a Land Acknowledgment entails, the video explains it well.

As I researched further, I learned that donations are vital for many projects that Indigenous leaders advocate for. The community needs people to physically attend protests, rallies, and legal proceedings to advocate for their rights to the land that was taken from them. Writing this entry and sharing what I've learned is just the beginning; I want to do more.

As you may know, I completed an intensive 8-month course called the Roots of Herbalism at the California School of Herbal Studies in 2022. During this course, I developed a deep bond with Mother Earth. In any relationship, building trust is essential. Trust takes time, dedication, and commitment, and it is crucial for nurturing an evolving relationship.

#### How you can take *meaningful* action now:

1. Respect tribal sovereignty and governance. If a Native nation has restricted access to its lands or a specific area, abide by their rules.

2. Prioritize visiting Native-managed places and using Native guides. Let park managers know you appreciate the inclusion of Indigenous voices in park materials and information.

3. **Leave places better than you found them by cleaning up your trash and any litter you see. Leave (do not take or disturb) natural "souvenirs" that are part of the local ecosystem.**

4. **Treat animals as relatives. Stay quiet and observe them from a distance.**

5. **Educate yourself on preventing the spread of invasive species and promoting plant health.**

6. **Use products (sunscreens, rash guards, insect repellents, etc.) that do not introduce harmful chemicals into our water or air.**

7. Research the Indigenous history of the lands you plan to visit. Find out which Native nations are nearby.

8. Use the Native name for specific places or landmarks. For example, say Mato Tipila or Bear Lodge instead of Devil's Tower.

9. Consider how your physical presence in a space may impact others.

*These suggestions (1-9) come from* [**NativeGov.org**](https://nativegov.org/).

#### How To Support Our Indigenous Community?

- If you can donate money, those funds are necessary to help advance these projects.
- Attend local protests, rallies, and legal proceedings to show your support.
- Watch the linked videos to learn how to begin advocating for our Indigenous community.
- Conduct your own research! YouTube can be a great resource.

I have **bolded** the action steps above that I connect with or actively practice.

I am eager to incorporate the others and become more involved.

Joye Braun from the [**Indigenous Environmental Network**](https://www.ienearth.org/) encourages us to attend protests in our local areas to challenge ourselves. During my herbalism training, one of our projects was creating a First Aid Kit for use in various situations. Our instructor mentioned that they utilized their kit in many protests, so it's time to put my knowledge into practice.

I will research upcoming protests in my county and surrounding areas to stand in solidarity with our Indigenous community as they fight to reclaim their land.

As you gather with your family and friends, please take a moment to reflect on how vital it is to support our Indigenous community now. I hope this message helps spread awareness about how you can take action in your community to restore our relationship with the land, nature, and Mother Earth.

We still have a lot of work to do!
