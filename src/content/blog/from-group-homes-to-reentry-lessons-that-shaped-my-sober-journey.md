There was a moment in my life where the trajectory could have gone very differently.

After moving through multiple group homes between the ages of 13 and 17, I was told something that stuck with me:

If I didn't get it together at Group Home 3… my next stop wouldn't be juvenile hall.

It would be jail.

That reality hit hard.

And somehow, something clicked.

---

#### **The Turning Point**

At Group Home 3, I made a decision — whether consciously or not — to work the program.

I leaned in.

I showed up.

I did the hard things.

And it paid off.

**What I Accomplished:**

- Graduated a year early
- Became Unit Leader — the highest level in the house
- Was cast as Anita in West Side Story (a role that pushed me to step into confidence and expression)
- And eventually received the news: it was time to start planning my return home

At the time, it felt like I had made it.

Like I had done everything right.

Like I was ready.

---

#### **The Reality of Going Home**

What I didn't realize was how mentally and emotionally challenging reentry would be.

I thought I was going back to a life that had paused for me.

But life doesn't pause.

It moves on.

When I came home, everything felt… off.

The friends I thought I would reconnect with?

Their parents didn't want them around me.

The relationships I thought I could rebuild?

They had moved on.

Everyone had new lives, new circles, new priorities.

And I was standing there, trying to fit back into a world that no longer had space for me.

I felt deeply alone.

---

#### **The Hidden Preparation**

Looking back now, I can see something I couldn't see then:

Those four years in group homes weren't just about survival.

They were preparation.

Because what came next required everything I had built — even if I didn't realize it at the time.

---

#### **Becoming Unhoused at 18**

Less than a year after returning home, I became unhoused.

There was no safety net.

No backup plan.

Just reality.

But there was one person who stepped in when everything else fell apart:

My junior high counselor.

She spoke with my family and learned I was not allowed to return home.

She offered me a place to stay — rent-free.

She took me to collect my belongings, which had been left outside on the porch.

I still remember that moment.

Standing there, looking at my life packed up and placed outside like it didn't matter.

It felt final.

It felt harsh.

And after everything I had worked through, I remember thinking:

This is the result? How is this fair?

---

#### **The Moment Everything Shifted**

That was the moment my rebellion took a turn.

Not the kind of rebellion I live today — but a different kind.

A survival-based rebellion.

A coping rebellion.

I bought a pack of cigarettes.

And a bottle of whiskey.

Because I thought: This is how people deal with life, right?

That moment marked the beginning of my alcohol addiction.

---

#### **What I Know Now**

I didn't have the tools.

I didn't have the support.

And I didn't have a space where I could process what I had been through while learning how to move forward.

I was trying to navigate:

- rejection
- identity loss
- loneliness
- instability
- and unprocessed trauma

All at once.

Without guidance.

---

#### **Why Beyond BARS Exists**

Everything I went through — the group homes, the reentry, the rejection, the addiction — led me to create something I wish I had back then: Beyond BARS Coaching — a space where system-impacted women don't just "get through" life…

They rebuild it.

**Through Beyond BARS, Sober Rebels learn to:**

- Create behavior change that actually sticks
- Take aligned action toward their goals
- Build resilience in the face of adversity
- Maintain sobriety while creating a life that feels worth staying sober for

Because sobriety isn't just about removing alcohol.

It's about building something better in its place.

---

#### **You Don't Have to Do This Alone**

If any part of this story resonates with you — the transition, the loneliness, the rebuilding — I want you to know:

You don't have to figure it out on your own.

**The Sober Rebel Club for Women** is a space where you can continue your journey with support, structure, and community.

**Inside, you'll get access to:**

- Weekly group calls for accountability and connection
- Foundational classroom-style courses
- A growing resource library
- In-person events and activities
- Text-based coaching for real-life support in real time

This is where we take the tools and actually apply them to real life.

---

If I could go back and talk to my younger self — standing there, collecting her belongings off the porch — I would tell her this:

You're not being rejected. You're being redirected.

Everything you're going through is going to become something powerful.

And one day, you're going to build something that helps other women find their way through it too.

---

Here's to your Sober Rebel journey.

Keep going.
