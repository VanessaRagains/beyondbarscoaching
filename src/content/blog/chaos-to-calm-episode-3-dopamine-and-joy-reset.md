### Science Made Simple

Let's start with the basics.

Dopamine is your brain's "feel-good" chemical. It's not happiness itself, but it's what motivates you to seek out things that bring pleasure, reward, or excitement. Think of it as your brain's gas pedal for motivation and desire.

Here's the problem: alcohol hijacks this system. Every time you drink, your brain releases a big surge of dopamine. But the more often this happens, the less sensitive your brain becomes. Over time, the things that used to bring you joy — a meal, a walk, a hug, even laughter — just don't feel as rewarding anymore.

This is why, in active drinking, people often feel flat, numb, or like they need alcohol just to feel "normal."

The good news? Sobriety allows your brain to reset its reward system. Within weeks of not drinking, your dopamine receptors begin to heal. And slowly but surely, you start to **feel joy** in everyday life again — but this time, it's real. It's sustainable. It's not borrowed from a bottle.

### Coaching Connection

Now, how do we weave this science into your recovery journey?

In the FAST Method, this week's focus is on Foundation again — specifically, building a foundation of joy.

When alcohol is no longer your primary source of dopamine, you get to rebuild your reward system with healthy, nourishing habits. This doesn't happen overnight — sometimes it takes weeks or months before you notice a shift. But every small, consistent practice lays the foundation for joy to return.

Here's a quick story. One of my clients once told me she couldn't imagine ever enjoying life without alcohol. But after a few weeks of tracking small wins, she started noticing dopamine in unexpected places — the smell of her morning coffee, the sound of her kid's laugh, the calm after yoga. These tiny sparks added up. And now, she describes her sobriety as *"color coming back into my world."*

### Practical Tool

**This week's tool is the Joy Audit.**

**Here's how it works:**

1. Each day, write down three moments — no matter how small — where you felt even a flicker of joy, peace, or satisfaction.
2. These could be as simple as: sunshine on your skin, finishing a workout, laughing at a silly video, or having a good conversation.
3. At the end of the week, look back at your list. You'll begin to see patterns — where real joy is hiding in your daily life.

This retrains your brain to pay attention to natural sources of dopamine — instead of chasing artificial highs from alcohol.
