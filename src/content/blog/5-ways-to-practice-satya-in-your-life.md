**I grew up avoiding conflict.** Talking back to the adults was never tolerated, so I learned early on to keep my feelings and imagination to myself. I expressed myself through playing make-believe, singing, and chatting with the creatures in the backyard. I would pretend that I was on an adventure out in nature. I needed shelter, food, and warmth so I would collect the necessary pieces around the house and back yard to set up my fort. I grabbed blankets, towels, and some tupperware my mom let me play with for collecting food (mud, leaves, sticks, flower petals, etc.).

In this space, I felt safe enough to express my honest self, my truest self. I would sing the words from "The Little Mermaid," "Look at this stuff, isn't it neat? Wouldn't you think my collection's complete? Wouldn't you think I was the girl, the girl who has everything..." or from Sound of Music, "I have confidence in sunshine, I have confidence in rain. I have confidence that spring will come again, besides what you see, I have confidence in me..." Singing has always connected me to my truest self.

But, there comes a time where you can't escape or pretend your way through life. I still have my incredible imagination, though!

Fast forward to today…

I've had my share of conflicts, hard conversations, and straight up yelling matches in my lifetime. Thinking about how the conflicts escalated between the adults in my life and between my sibling and I, and a very abusive partner in my past, I remembered why I avoided any form of conflict in the first place.

Let's explore a piece of #Yoga called #Satya. Satya means practicing truthful thinking, speech, and action in order to promote reconciliation and peace in myself and among others.

That's a powerful practice!

After finally leaving a very abusive relationship, I made an "Argument Policy" with myself and others. I no longer tolerate name calling, cursing, or yelling when in a conflict or disagreement. Believe it or not, you can argue gracefully. Plus, when the name-calling starts, or cursing, the argument has lost its purpose to resolve anything and has become a "who can hurt who worse" fiasco. There have been too many heart crushing words that have been directed at me or have left my mouth, and I refuse to continue this old pattern.

Now, I *practice* #Satya as a tool for navigating how I choose to think, speak, and in my actions. If I feel *any* hesitation towards a thought, through my words or actions, I will pause and reflect before I make any commitments to myself or others. Then, I am not overcommitting or saying that I will do something that I really don't want to do. The simple act of pausing allows me to make confident decisions and increases my ability to follow-through on my commitments to myself and others — a commitment to Satya.

#### Ways to practice truthful thinking, speech and deed.

1. **Daily Check-ins with yourself.** I literally ask myself, "How are you doing today?" just like you would a dear friend. Then, answer yourself honestly.

2. **Identifying negative (untrue) Self-talk.** Negative self-talk can come from something that occurred in childhood that you have carried with you or a terrible habit. Catch yourself, even if you complete your thought. BUT, you immediately reframe it into something positive or that you like about yourself. Eventually, eliminating these negative thoughts whenever they creep in.

3. **Are you being real or are you being nice?** Be #Honest with yourself. Also, be gentle with yourself, especially if this is brand new to you. Be aware of when you feel the urge to speak nicely vs speaking your truth. Remember, if we can argue gracefully, we can craft our words to be real vs. nice.

*Side note:* I was that nice human. Always kind, said please and thank you, and anticipated when folks would need my help. Shifting to be "real" in my speech vs. "nice" is still a challenge. However, overtime, I am finding that it is not as intimidating as I thought it was. The relationship that I am building with myself grows stronger every day, especially after making this shift into speaking my truth. It's worth it!

4. **Saying what you mean the first time.** Have you ever had to go back on a promise you made? Ever think about how that would have been different if you were honest about your intention the first time? For me, this comes down to energy. I have my daily check-ins and if I am asked to do something or switch plans around, my answer will depend greatly on how my energy levels are in the moment. When I commit to something now, my follow-through has greatly improved because I make an honest commitment. In times where I am unable to assist in the request, I have been practicing saying no without internally criticizing myself. I'm still a work in progress, but I get better every day.

5. **True Self-expression.** Are you expressing your true self? I see this as wearing what you want to wear, not what society or your parents/grandparents say you should wear, or anyone for that matter. For me to fully express myself, I made the appointment to finally start my dreadlock journey. I've learned a lot about myself, my soul, and my true desires that I want to experience in my lifetime. Some folks decide to wear artistic makeup, others decorate their bodies with tattoos. The choice is yours and yours alone. PS. Self-expressive tattoos are next for me.

Throughout this process, I have been able to reconnect with that "Little Human" who sang out loud, built forts, and who made mud and leaf soup to remember how scary it can be to not live your truth and keep it hidden. Today, I embrace my authentic soul, living my truth by practicing Satya.

And, yes, I still sing. In the car, in the camper van, or whenever I find a place that hosts karaoke.
