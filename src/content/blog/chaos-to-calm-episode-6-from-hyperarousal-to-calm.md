### Science Made Simple

So, what is **hyperarousal**?

It's when your nervous system is stuck in **high alert mode.** Your heart races, your thoughts spin, your body feels tight or restless. It's the feeling of being ready to fight or flee — even when there's no real danger.

Alcohol plays a tricky role here. At first, it might feel like a drink takes the edge off. But behind the scenes, alcohol is actually **amplifying stress hormones like cortisol and adrenaline.**

That means when the buzz wears off, your nervous system is left even more activated. It's why you might wake up at 3 a.m. with a pounding heart or feel edgy and anxious the next day.

Sobriety allows your stress-response system to finally settle. Within weeks, your body begins to relearn how to come down from hyperarousal naturally. The more time you give it, the stronger those calm pathways become.

### Coaching Connection

This week in the FAST Method, our focus is on **Tools.**

When you're in hyperarousal, you can't just "think" your way to calm. You need tools that help your body feel safe enough to shift gears.

For me, one of the first tools that worked was breathwork. I remember noticing how my chest felt tight and my mind wouldn't stop racing. A few minutes of slow breathing helped signal to my nervous system: *"You're safe. You can stand down."*

Other tools might be grounding practices, movement, or sensory resets — like listening to calming music or holding something warm in your hands.

The key is to practice these tools regularly so your brain starts linking stress to calm instead of stress to chaos.

### Practical Tool

This week's tool is the **Calm Toolkit.**

Here's how to build it:

1. Make a list of 3–5 simple actions that help your body shift gears. Examples: breathing exercises, a walk outside, a grounding object, stretching, or a calming playlist.
2. Keep your toolkit visible — in your journal, on a sticky note, or in your phone.
3. When hyperarousal hits, don't overthink it. Just pick one tool and use it for 2–5 minutes.

Every time you reach for a calm tool instead of alcohol, you reinforce your brain's natural ability to regulate.
