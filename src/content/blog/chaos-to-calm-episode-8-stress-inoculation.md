### Science Made Simple

So, what does that mean?

Your nervous system works a lot like a muscle. If you avoid all stress, that "muscle" stays weak. If you're constantly flooded with stress, it burns out. But if you give your body **small doses of controlled stress, followed by recovery,** your nervous system actually grows stronger.

This is called **stress inoculation.** Think of it like a vaccine — small amounts of exposure build resilience over time.

Examples of controlled stress include:

- Brief cold exposure, like a splash of cold water on your face.
- A short, challenging workout.
- Timed deep breathing, where you stretch your limits just a little.

Here's the key: it's not the stress itself that builds resilience. It's your nervous system learning to come back to calm afterwards. Sobriety makes this process possible again — because your brain isn't numbed or hijacked by alcohol.

It wasn't about the push-up or the cold water. It was about teaching my body: *"You can handle discomfort without alcohol. You're safe."*

That's the heart of resilience.

### Practical Tool

This week's practice is the **Calm Muscle Tracker.**

Here's how to use it:

1. Choose one small stress exercise each day. Examples: cold splash, quick workout, timed breath hold, or a mental challenge like a puzzle.
2. Notice how your body feels before the exercise. Write it down: *"I feel anxious, tense, restless."*
3. Do the practice.
4. Afterwards, notice how your body feels: *"I feel calmer, more focused, lighter."*

This builds evidence for your brain that stress isn't the enemy — it's training ground for resilience.
