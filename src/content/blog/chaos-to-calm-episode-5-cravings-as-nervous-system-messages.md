### Science Made Simple

Let's start here: what is a craving, really?

Biologically, a craving is your brain's way of saying: *"Something feels off. I need relief."*

When you drink alcohol regularly, your brain wires itself to believe that alcohol is the fastest way to get that relief. The craving isn't about the drink itself — it's about your nervous system asking for regulation.

For example:

- If you're stressed, your brain might crave alcohol because it remembers that quick numbing effect.
- If you're lonely, it might crave alcohol because it remembers false connection.
- If you're tired, it might crave alcohol because your brain associates it with shutting down.

But here's the thing: cravings are just misdirected messages. They're not failures. They're signals from your body saying, *"I need something."* And sobriety gives you the chance to answer those messages with healthier tools.

### Coaching Connection

This week in the FAST Method, we focus on Support.

Support is what helps you interrupt cravings and rewire those old pathways. You don't have to "white knuckle" your way through them — you can build rituals and support systems that teach your brain new ways to respond.

I'll share a quick story. One of my clients used to panic when cravings hit, convinced it meant she was about to relapse. But once she started writing down what was happening in her body — and reaching out to her support network — she realized the cravings always passed. They weren't signs of failure; they were signals.

Now she sees cravings as *"red flags pointing me toward what I actually need."* And that shift has been life-changing.

### Practical Tool

This week's tool is the Craving Pause Ritual.

**Next time a craving shows up, walk yourself through this three-step checklist:**

1. Stop + Breathe. Take three slow breaths to ground yourself.
2. Move. Do something physical — walk, stretch, shake it out.
3. Reflect. Ask: *What's this craving really about? Am I stressed, lonely, bored, tired? What do I actually need?*

Then — meet the real need. Call a friend, drink water, rest, journal, or move your body.

Every time you pause instead of pouring, you're carving a brand-new rebel pathway in your brain.
