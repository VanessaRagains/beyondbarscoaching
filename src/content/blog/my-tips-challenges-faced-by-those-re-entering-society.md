Imagine being incarcerated, in rehab, or living in a group home for years. You've been told it's time to prepare for your release back into society. The emotions are complex: excitement, fear, and a sense of overwhelming uncertainty. You've been away from family and friends for so long, and while you're eager to reconnect and experience "real food" after months of unappetizing meals, you're also scared of the unknowns ahead. The structure, support, and accountability you had in the institution are now gone, and you're left to navigate a world that may feel foreign and unpredictable.

When the day of your release comes, you gather your belongings — some of which you may not have seen in years — and step through that final gate. The feeling of freedom is surreal, but reality sets in quickly. You have a lot of work ahead of you. You have to prove not only to the system that sent you away but also to yourself that you're ready to create a new chapter in your life.

Let's dive into the key challenges faced during this transition and how you can overcome them.

1. **Establishing Healthy Boundaries and Relationships**

One of the biggest lessons I wish I'd learned sooner is how to set boundaries with family, friends, and others in my life. Coming home from a group home, I didn't realize how vital it would be to define what behaviors I would tolerate, not just from others, but from myself.

Setting boundaries starts with self-awareness. For example:

**Constructive conflict:** I learned that arguments should stay respectful. No name-calling, cursing, or yelling. If an argument crosses those lines, it stops being productive and becomes a battle of attacks.

**Self-check-ins:** Regularly check in with yourself to assess your energy and emotional state. If you're feeling drained or obligated to do something, saying no is OK. Being honest with yourself builds trust and strengthens your relationship with yourself.

**Food choices:** What you eat matters. Healthy food fuels your body and mind, affecting your mood, energy, and interactions with others. I've learned that maintaining balanced blood sugar is key to staying grounded and focused.

Setting clear boundaries and making healthy choices are key to creating a stable foundation as you re-enter society.

2. **Finding Safe and Reliable Housing**

After being released, I had a place to stay temporarily, but it wasn't long before I found myself without a roof over my head. I bounced from one place to another, each time overstaying my welcome. The reality of being homeless was starting to sink in.

That's when I got an unexpected call from my junior high school counselor. She asked if I was OK and where I was staying. She had been in touch with my family and knew what had happened. Without hesitation, she offered me a place to stay. She had a spare bedroom, and I could help around the house. It was the first stable living environment I had experienced since my release.

Unfortunately, not everyone has a counselor or support system like I did. Finding safe, reliable housing is one of the greatest challenges people face after release. Housing instability can push many back into the system or, worse, into homelessness. Everyone deserves a safe place to call home, and that's why I'm committed to helping those who need it by networking with shelters and offering resources to help people find stable housing.

3. **Building a Dependable Support System**

A key part of the Beyond BARS Coaching program is creating a reliable support system. When I was in transition, I didn't have the structure of a group home or rehab facility to lean on. That's why it's crucial to establish connections with people who support your goals and well-being.

As you start rebuilding your life, focus on quality, not quantity, when it comes to relationships. Your support system might include counselors, teachers, mentors, and friends who share your values and aspirations. Remember, the key is to be selective. Surround yourself with people who are trustworthy, positive, and invested in your success.

4. **Holding Yourself Accountable for Healthy Habit Change**

Transitioning back into society can be overwhelming, and it's easy to fall into old habits or make unhealthy choices. When I moved in with my junior high counselor, I had the structure I needed to start building healthy habits. But when she retired, I struggled. I fell into an abusive relationship, turned to drugs and alcohol, and went through multiple jobs and living situations. It was messy, to say the least.

Then yoga came into my life. It was a game-changer. It offered me structure, a path to self-awareness, and the opportunity to heal. That's when I invested in a 200-hour Yoga Teacher Training (YTT) program. The journey wasn't easy, but it was transformative. Through yoga, I began to address my negative self-talk, release the tension stored in my body, and rebuild my relationship with myself.

This is where Beyond BARS Coaching comes in. As a client, you'll build a personal yoga practice and work with a certified Health and Life Coach. Together, we'll identify the healthy habits you need to create a life with purpose and direction.

5. **Navigating Education and Career Pathways**

When you're ready to take the next step, it's time to focus on your education and career goals. At Beyond BARS Coaching, we create a roadmap — a step-by-step plan to help you reach your goals.

A major resource for me has been LinkedIn. Building a professional LinkedIn profile and connecting with people who can support your growth is invaluable. Through LinkedIn, I'll contact my network and introduce clients to people who can open doors to opportunities in their chosen fields.

As you develop trusting relationships with yourself, you'll gain the confidence to build meaningful connections with others.
