**I remember** the conversation that I had in my head as I debated whether I was going to eliminate alcohol from my life or not. It was December 9, 2020. The push was because a family member was brave enough to address their addiction and start treatment. At first, I stayed sober to support this incredible human who put their life on hold because continuing on was not going to end well. But there was a part of me that desperately wanted to live a sober life too. However, somehow I knew that it wouldn't be an easy road.

Today, I checked my "I Am Sober" app to see how many days I have been sober. The app shows you down to the very second of your time in sobriety so it is very satisfying to check from time to time. I have been sober for 1,226 days. That's 3 years and 4 months of ZERO alcohol. It seems like a long time, but it still feels hard to me. Some days, a craving or trigger will sneak up on me. I've been able to breathe through the emotions, or use a holistic remedy to support me in those moments. They pass, eventually.

Choosing sobriety is a *rebellious* act and a commitment that you choose to follow through on every single day. One thing that has made a huge difference for me is the trusting relationship that I am building with myself, sharpening my mental health/resilience, and creating and maintaining healthy boundaries.

**These are the 3 pieces of Personal Development:**

*1. Creating and maintaining healthy boundaries with yourself and others.*

*2. Sharpening your Mental Health.*

*3. Building a trusting relationship with yourself.*

Each piece benefits from one another.

**Creating and maintaining healthy boundaries with yourself and others may look like** not hanging out at the local bars anymore.

**Sharpening your mental health can look like** daily check ins with yourself. Literally asking yourself how you feel. Learning to identify when you feel triggered and addressing the situation right away.

**Putting it all together**, helps you build a trusting relationship with yourself. Being able to trust yourself to say "No" when you really don't want to do something. Not overbooking yourself to accommodate clients, or trusting yourself to make healthy choices with your meals each week.

I never realized how vital Personal Development was until I ditched the alcohol for good. Each time I maintained a boundary, practiced my daily check-ins with myself, or made commitments to myself and others, I was building my Personal Development muscle.

Think about how you can practice Personal Development in your life using the Coaching exercise below.

**Personal Development Coaching Exercise:**

*1. What is 1 boundary you need to create in your life? Who is it for? What will maintaining this boundary create in your life?*

*2. How would having 1-3 daily check-ins with yourself help you sharpen your mental health? You can set these reminders as alarms in your phone as you implement this new habit.*

*3. What are 3 ways you can start building a trusting relationship with yourself? What needs to change/shift/adjust?*

*4.* ***BONUS:*** *When will you commit to implementing these pieces into your life? What areas will be difficult to implement now?*
