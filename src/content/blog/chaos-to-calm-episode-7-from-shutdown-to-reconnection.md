### Science Made Simple

So, what exactly is shutdown?

When your nervous system feels overwhelmed, it can swing the other way — instead of overdrive, it goes into freeze or fawn.

- Freeze looks like zoning out, going numb, or feeling paralyzed.
- Fawn looks like people-pleasing, avoiding conflict, or over-accommodating others at the expense of yourself.

Alcohol often reinforces these patterns. It numbs emotions, dulls sensations, and helps you disconnect instead of dealing with stress. Over time, your nervous system learns: *"When it's too much, shut down."*

But sobriety gives your brain the chance to relearn connection. As your nervous system heals, it begins to send signals again — signals of emotion, of sensation, of presence. At first, this can feel overwhelming. But with practice, you build the capacity to stay connected instead of checking out.

That's the shift: from shutdown to reconnection.

### Coaching Connection

This week in the FAST Method, we're focusing on Support.

Why support? Because reconnection doesn't happen in isolation. When you've trained your body to numb or fawn, you need safe people, practices, and spaces to practice connection.

I'll share a story. A client once told me she felt like sobriety made her "too sensitive." She cried easily, felt emotions more deeply, and wasn't sure what to do with it all. Together, we reframed it: this wasn't weakness — it was her nervous system *coming back online.*

With the right support — journaling, grounding, and leaning on her recovery group — she learned to stay present with those emotions instead of numbing them. Today, she says: *"I'd rather feel everything than nothing at all."*

That's the power of reconnection.

### Practical Tool

This week's tool is the Reconnect Ritual.

**Here's how it works:**

1. Pause and name what you feel. Even if it's just: *"I feel numb"* or *"I feel anxious."*
2. Take three grounding breaths.
3. Do one action to reconnect: stretch, journal, call a safe person, or take a mindful walk.
4. Reflect: *Before this, I felt ____. After this, I feel ____.*

This teaches your nervous system that emotions and sensations are safe to experience — and that you can move through them without shutting down.
